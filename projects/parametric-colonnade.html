The Colonnade

Washington and Lee University, Lexington, Virginia. Five Greek Revival buildings behind one continuous white portico, drawn here entirely from code: every column, opening and cornice comes from the dimensions in the parameter list, in feet. Change a number and the sheet redraws.

Drag any slider to regenerate the drawing. On a narrow screen, scroll the sheet sideways.

What this is

A parametric model: the drawing is not traced, it is computed. Column positions come from bay width and bay count, the entablature depth follows column diameter, the pediment follows a roof pitch in rise-per-twelve, and the brick course count falls out of wall height at 2 3/4 inches a course. Nothing is drawn by hand, so any dimension you change propagates through the whole sheet.

The defaults are proportioned by eye from the Greek Revival originals, not measured from the buildings. Treat the geometry as a study model, not survey data.

The five halls

  1. 01Newcomb Hall1881
  2. 02Payne Hall1831
  3. 03Washington Hall1824
  4. 04Chavis Hall1842
  5. 05Tucker Hall1935

West to east as you face the Colonnade from the Front Lawn. Washington Hall carries the cupola and the statue of George Washington. Chavis Hall was named Robinson Hall until 2018, when the university renamed it for John Chavis. Tucker Hall was rebuilt after a fire; the row was declared a National Historic Landmark in 1972.
