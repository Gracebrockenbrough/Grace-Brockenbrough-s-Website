What is parametric here

Every surface is generated, not modelled by hand. Wall planes come from the footprint, the roof is built from the pitch as a rise in twelve, window openings are spaced evenly across each elevation, and the rafter length that sets roof area is sqrt(144 + pitch^2) / 12 times the run. Change one number and the geometry, the takeoff and the STL all move together.

The takeoff uses real construction units: a square is 100 square feet of siding or roofing, asphalt shingles run three bundles to the square, and studs are counted at 16 inches on center plus corners. Quantities are a study estimate, not a construction order.

Printing it

Export writes a binary STL in millimetres, sized at the scale you pick, oriented Z-up the way slicers expect. At 1:100 a typical two-storey house lands near a 100 mm footprint, so it prints on any small bed; 1:50 doubles it.

Parts overlap rather than being boolean-joined, which every common slicer unions on load. Walls are solid: hollow them with your slicer's vase or infill settings rather than in the model.
